# Scientific interpretation and reviewer-facing disposition

## Bottom line

The deterministic extension succeeds scientifically, but it does not support a
large correction-of-record claim. It converts the reviewer question “does the
collector-zero defect move a published quantity?” into an exact, bounded answer.

For the 14 projects that actually entered the anchor's downstream summary, the
design-based plug-in candidate-exclusion share is 1.15% under the
source-supported endpoint and 3.70% when unresolved units are included as a
separate sensitivity endpoint. The corresponding pooled all-commits rate can
move upward by at most 0.16 or 0.52 percentage points when the unidentified
candidate-row mean is set to its mathematical maximum of one.

The aggregate consequence is therefore modest. It is not scientifically honest
to describe the result as a corrected testability rate or as evidence that the
anchor's conclusions are overturned. The stronger finding is heterogeneity:
the supported-only project-level maximum upward envelope reaches 3.20 percentage
points (`ansj_seg`), while the supported-or-unresolved envelope reaches 14.51
points (`Bukkit`) and is explicitly driven by unresolved status rather than
observed runtime behavior.

## Exact answer to the skeptical reviewer

The study is no longer only a warning about a possible measurement defect. It
reconstructs all 66 published downstream project rows, links every one of the
3,393 audit-frame rows exactly to the frozen anchor tables, and quantifies the
defect's sensitivity for the 14-project downstream overlap. Under the frozen
assumptions, pooled movement is bounded and comparatively small, but individual
projects can have materially wider denominator exposure. Runtime outcomes remain
unidentified, so the analysis bounds robustness rather than adjudicating the
anchor's substantive conclusions.

## Positioning consequence

This result strengthens the paper as a measurement-method and robustness paper,
not as a bug report and not as a record correction. The most defensible novelty
claim is the combination of:

1. a witness-bearing three-state audit protocol;
2. exact published-code and row-level provenance; and
3. a pre-frozen consequence analysis that distinguishes design-based estimates,
   finite-population confidence sets, and no-exchangeability bounds.

The evidence does not justify the earlier proposed sentence that 54–65% of the
published rate sequence consists of potentially false zeros. Those percentages
describe source-evidence prevalence in the audit frame, not the proportion of
the complete published rate denominator, and four large frame projects never
entered the downstream summary. The correct pooled denominator-exposure values
are 1.15–3.70% under the frozen plug-in endpoints.

No journal acceptance probability is recomputed here. Any numerical acceptance
forecast would be strategy opinion, not an output of this deterministic analysis.
